---
title: Collaboration Philosophy
category: philosophy
tags: [collaboration, teamwork, communication, code review, process]
---

I think the best quality work comes from tight collaboration between developers and QA, not from throwing builds over a wall. I prefer being involved early — during design discussions, PR reviews, and architecture decisions — rather than waiting until something is "ready to test."

Code review is where I learn the most and contribute the most outside of my own code. I look for testability, edge cases, and failure modes that the author might not have considered. I try to be specific in my feedback — pointing to exact lines, suggesting alternatives, and explaining why something matters.

I value clear, asynchronous communication. Good commit messages, well-structured PRs with context, and documentation that answers the questions people actually have. I've seen too many projects where the only documentation is "ask Dave" — and Dave left six months ago.

When working on a team, I prefer small, frequent merges over large feature branches that sit open for weeks. Smaller changes are easier to review, easier to test, and easier to revert if something goes wrong.
